# Department Review 11 — Public Alpha & Player Research

## Purpose

A technically complete game still needs evidence from people who did not build it.

## Sample

Start small:
- new Roblox players to this experience;
- experienced Roblox players;
- solo joiners;
- friend pairs/groups;
- at least some mobile-first players.

Avoid coaching participants unless the study specifically tests tutorial wording.

## Observe, do not lead

Record:
- first confusion;
- first laugh/excitement;
- first death;
- whether death is understood;
- whether player chooses another round;
- whether player notices other survivors;
- whether player uses social features naturally.

## Interview questions after play

Ask:
- What was the goal?
- What killed you?
- What was your favorite moment?
- What felt unfair?
- What did you ignore on screen?
- Which map do you remember?
- Would you play another round?
- Would this be better with friends?
- What looked cheap or unfinished?
- What would you remove?

Do not ask leading questions such as "Did you like the new lighting?"

## Behavioral metrics

Track:
- first-round completion;
- first survival;
- second-round conversion;
- 10-minute retention;
- quit-after-death;
- solo vs multi;
- hazard-specific death rate;
- invite/share funnel;
- device/FPS.

## Synthesis

For each issue classify:
- comprehension;
- control;
- fairness;
- pacing;
- visual;
- audio;
- social;
- performance;
- progression.

Prioritize repeated observed behavior over isolated preference.

## Alpha exit criteria

Before broader release:
- no repeated P0/P1 confusion;
- no common unexplained death pattern;
- mobile players can control comfortably;
- second-round conversion acceptable relative to observed reasons;
- social prompts do not feel intrusive;
- no economy/monetization fairness complaints caused by actual design;
- major performance outliers understood.

## Research principle

What players **do** is primary.
What players **say** explains why.
Both matter.
